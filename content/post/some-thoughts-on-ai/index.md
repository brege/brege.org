I've thought a lot about Tristan Harris' comparison of AI's advent being the intellectual equivalent of distilling thousands of back-breaking manhours into a single barrel of crude oil. 


I think of the image of Chinese and indentured immigrants carving tunnels through the Rockies with elementary explosives and fewer fundamental rights used during the expansion of the intercontinental railroad in one century with the construction of the US's interstate system by diesel arms, better labor protections, and fewer casualties in the next century over.  I've traveled via the Empire Builder by rail in the north and cut through three latitudes of interstate in that direction too. The Rockies, no matter who or by what means were used to cut them is truly one of the most impressive engineering feats mankind has ever accomplished.

When it comes to the current moment, the fatigue of social media and the death of the open web as we know it, Tristan reminds us that we don't have to rush to this ultimate end. We do not have to force the entanglement of AI into all of the government's systems the way we've foolishly done for emergency services through X, de-facto social credit through the FANGMAN, or the missed opportunity to have the USPS provide email accounts for the cost of a stamp. 

I wish there was greater control. I wish this industry wasn't captained by fractional trillionaires and was instead helmed the way the Manhattan Project, Bell Labs, or CERN's role in the creation of the internet.

What worries me most of all isn't the death of links. Social media and giant walls of phones--Click Farms--in the developing world already did. The billionaires decided they were okay with slave labor if they could dilute the negative reviews their companies get with an artificially inflated grade provided on the backs of people who will never pay for their device or app. It's the biggest open lie in both of their app stores. I try to remember this when I read reviews on the Mozilla Add-on's page or other community hub, where click farming isn't lucrative.

While LLM's are trained on a wide range of literature, we are promised there are gaurdrails in place for truly dangerous things. Ominously, we are all left to imagine what protections these are for. Is it bullying and self harm? Is it development of deadly weapons and intercontintal ballistics? All nations are willing to pay top dollar for silicon anyway, and they aren't going to use Claude or ChatGPT to design "the bomb". The intelligence apparatus will no doubt let us down eventually, having proven none of it effective.

When I went to college, it seemed one in three people were going for a psychology degree. Where did all of these people go? Ads. Ads and marketing. Engagement. "For the clicks."  Salesmen dressing up their profession as "influencers" selling more soaps with a prize inside. I am Jack's complete lack of self-control. The pure corruption of sports from an embedded gambling cuthula, knowing no limits to its wickedness, is robbing the last vestige of wealth from the baby boomers to noisy slots, indignified death homes, and turning their houses into do-you-own-chores motels.

The pointing and inference of AI on psychological works, and the places where it can make large breakthroughs and test hypotheses out on human subjects gives me the greatest worry. People may well shift on put the pick axes down for a finely tuned remote controlled tractor.

**In my view, the laize-faire handling of psychological experimentation by AI on people must be provided no smaller than a cancer warnings on cigarettes.**

Physicists, including many of those I've been a student of, often talk about turning away DoD positions or national lab appointments if their work could be used to kill people. This sobering scenario of psycoloigcal snowballing, in my view, has chilled a lot of my interest in AI and data science at large.

It's this disgusting thirst of gamifying people, tricking us to surrender our agency, advocating that our misuses and lack of impulse control is a responsibility forever abdicated by the only powers who can remedy change from central authority. These various terminals of bullshit that we have to deal with every. single. day. *Thank you person who sharpies the no-sound button on gas pump ads*.
